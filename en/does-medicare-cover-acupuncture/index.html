How this is calculated

Medicare Part B covers acupuncture, including dry needling, for one condition only: chronic low back pain, meaning pain that has lasted 12 weeks or longer, has no known cause such as cancer that has spread or an inflammatory or infectious disease, and is not linked to surgery or pregnancy. Medicare covers up to 12 treatments in 90 days; if you show improvement, it covers 8 more, for a maximum of 20 in a 12-month period. Without improvement, further sessions are not covered and you pay 100% of them. Each covered session costs you 20% of the Medicare-approved amount after the $283 Part B deductible: a first 15-minute session is approved at $48 nationally, so $10 for you; 12 sessions leave you $115 and the full 20, $192. Medicare cannot pay licensed acupuncturists directly: the treatment must come from a doctor or another provider, such as a nurse practitioner or physician assistant, who holds an accredited master's or doctoral degree in acupuncture or Oriental Medicine and a full state license to practice acupuncture.

Who qualifies

The medicare.gov acupuncture page defines chronic low back pain in three parts. It has lasted 12 weeks or longer. It has no known cause: it is not related to cancer that has spread, or to an inflammatory or infectious disease. And it is not associated with surgery or pregnancy. If your back pain fits, Part B pays for acupuncture, dry needling included; if it does not, or if the pain is elsewhere, the sessions are not covered.

Twelve, then twenty

The number of covered sessions works in two steps. Up to 12 treatments in 90 days are covered from the start. If you show improvement, 8 more are covered, for a maximum of 20 in a 12-month period. If you are not improving, Medicare does not cover further treatment, and you pay 100% of any session you continue.

What a session costs where you live

The approved amount follows the physician fee schedule and its local price areas: one session is approved at $54 in Manhattan and $44 in Alabama, against $48 at national rates. The amount priced here is the first 15 minutes of a session; a longer session is billed higher, and your 20% rises with it. Choose your state and area in the estimator to see your own rate.

Medigap and the twenty sessions

A Medigap policy pays after Original Medicare on each covered session. Plan G pays the whole 20% once the deductible is met, so 20 sessions cost you $0; Plan K pays half, leaving $96, and Plan L three quarters, leaving $48. Sessions beyond the covered ones, or for another condition, are not Medicare-approved, so no Medigap letter pays them.

Who can give the treatment

Medicare cannot pay licensed acupuncturists directly. A doctor, or another provider such as a nurse practitioner or physician assistant, can be paid if he or she has a master's or doctoral degree in acupuncture or Oriental Medicine from a school accredited by the Accreditation Commission for Acupuncture and Herbal Medicine, and a current, full, active and unrestricted license to practice acupuncture in the state where you are treated. Ask the clinic who will bill Medicare before the first session.

Back pain care Medicare covers otherwise

For back pain that does not meet the acupuncture rule, other Part B services follow the usual deductible and 20%: physical therapy, a chiropractic spine adjustment to correct a subluxation, or an MRI of the lower back when your doctor orders it. Massage therapy is on the list of services Original Medicare does not cover. A Medicare Advantage plan must cover the low back pain acupuncture benefit and may add more; read its Summary of Benefits.

Questions people on Medicare ask

Does Medicare cover acupuncture for neck pain or arthritis?

No. Part B pays for acupuncture only when it treats chronic low back pain that meets Medicare's definition: 12 weeks or longer, no known cause, not associated with surgery or pregnancy. Acupuncture for neck pain, knee arthritis, headaches or any other condition is not covered by Original Medicare, and you pay the full price. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer extra benefits; ask the plan.

Can a licensed acupuncturist bill Medicare?

Not directly. Medicare cannot pay licensed acupuncturists for their services. The treatment has to be given by a doctor or by another health care provider, such as a nurse practitioner or physician assistant, who has both a master's or doctoral degree in acupuncture or Oriental Medicine from an accredited school and a current, full, active and unrestricted license to practice acupuncture in your state.

What happens after 12 acupuncture sessions?

If your low back pain is improving, Medicare covers 8 more sessions, up to 20 in 12 months; at national rates those 8 add $77 to your 20%. If you are not improving, Medicare stops paying and any further session is entirely yours. Ask the provider to document your progress before the 12th session.
