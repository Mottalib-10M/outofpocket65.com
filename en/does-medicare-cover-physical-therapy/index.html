Does Medicare cover physical therapy?

Yes. Part B pays for medically necessary outpatient physical therapy once a provider certifies you need it, and there is no yearly limit on what Medicare pays. The estimator prices a course of sessions for your area at 2026 rates.

How this is calculated

Medicare Part B covers outpatient physical therapy that is medically necessary, after your doctor, a nurse practitioner, a clinical nurse specialist or a physician assistant certifies that you need it, and it sets no limit on how much it pays for that therapy in a calendar year. You pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount once the $283 Part B deductible is met. At national 2026 rates, the first evaluation of moderate complexity is approved $98, so your share is $20, and a typical session of three 15-minute units of exercise is approved $87, leaving you $17. A course of twelve sessions therefore costs you about $209 on top of the evaluation. The number people often confuse with a cap is the KX threshold: $2,480 in 2026 for physical therapy and speech-language pathology combined. Once billed therapy passes it, Medicare keeps paying, but the therapist must add a modifier confirming that the care is still medically necessary and document why. Medigap Plan G pays the 20% in full after the deductible; prices also vary with the local cost area of the clinic.

What Part B pays for

Physical therapy, in Medicare's words, helps restore or improve movement after an injury, an illness or surgery, and can also help maintain your current function or slow a decline. That last part matters: therapy does not have to promise a full recovery to be covered. What it needs is a provider's certification that it is medically necessary, and an outpatient setting such as a therapist's office or clinic.

Evaluation first, then sessions

A course of therapy starts with an evaluation, billed once, and continues with sessions made of timed units of exercise and hands-on work. The table prices the most common pattern at national 2026 rates: an evaluation of moderate complexity, then sessions of three 15-minute units.

If therapy is the first Part B care of your year, the deductible is paid out of the first claims. The evaluation then costs you $98, and the twelve sessions that follow $357, because what remains of the deductible is taken from them before the 20% applies.

The KX threshold, explained without the jargon

The dollar figure attached to outpatient therapy, $2,480 in 2026 for physical therapy and speech-language pathology together, works as a checkpoint. When the year's approved amounts reach it, the therapist must mark each further claim with the KX modifier, a statement that the therapy is still medically necessary, and keep notes that back it up. Occupational therapy has its own threshold of $2,480.

For you, nothing changes in price above the threshold: the session is still approved at the same amount and you still pay 20%. What matters is the therapist's documentation, so a long course of therapy, after a joint replacement for example, is worth discussing with the clinic before the threshold is reached. Clinic location and supplemental coverage

The fee for each unit is adjusted to the local cost of running a practice. Twelve sessions with the deductible met cost you $234 in Manhattan and $196 in Alabama, against $209 at national rates. Choose your state and price area in the estimator to see the figure for your clinic.

A Medigap policy changes the share far more than location does. Plan G pays all of the 20%, which brings the twelve sessions to $0 once the deductible is met. Plan K pays half, leaving $105. With a Medicare Advantage plan, the plan sets its own cost sharing, at least as broad in coverage as Original Medicare; enter your plan's figures in the estimator.

Therapy at home is a separate benefit

If you are homebound and receive physical therapy from a Medicare-certified home health agency, the visits fall under the home health benefit, and you pay nothing for them. The 20% described on this page applies to outpatient therapy, the kind you travel to.

Questions people on Medicare ask

Does Medicare stop paying for physical therapy after a certain amount?

No. The $2,480 figure for 2026 is a threshold, not a cap. Above it, your therapist adds the KX modifier to the claim to confirm the therapy remains medically necessary, and keeps the records that support it. Medicare continues to pay its 80% as long as the care is justified, so the threshold changes the paperwork, not your price.

How many sessions fit under the KX threshold?

At national 2026 rates, one evaluation plus about 27 standard sessions stay under the $2,480 threshold, if no speech therapy is billed the same year. Speech-language pathology counts toward the same amount, while occupational therapy has its own threshold of $2,480. The mini tool recalculates the count when you enter speech therapy already billed.

Who has to certify that I need physical therapy?

Your doctor or another health care provider, which Medicare says includes a nurse practitioner, a clinical nurse specialist or a physician assistant. The certification is what makes the therapy medically necessary in Medicare's eyes. Without it, Part B does not cover the sessions, even when they are given by a licensed physical therapist who accepts Medicare.
