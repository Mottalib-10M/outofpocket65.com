Does Medicare cover dialysis?

Yes, for end-stage renal disease: Part B pays outpatient and home dialysis, Part A dialysis during a hospital stay. The estimator counts a month of treatments at the 2026 national base rate.

How this is calculated

Medicare covers dialysis for people with end-stage renal disease (ESRD), permanent kidney failure that needs dialysis or a transplant. To get the full ESRD benefit with Original Medicare you need both parts: Part B pays outpatient treatments in a Medicare-certified dialysis facility or at home, home dialysis training, equipment and supplies, and the drugs used for dialysis; Part A pays dialysis while you are an inpatient. Medicare usually covers 3 hemodialysis treatments a week, or the equivalent peritoneal dialysis. CMS pays facilities a base rate of $281.71 per treatment in 2026, before adjustments for each patient and facility, so your 20% is about $56 a treatment once the Part B deductible is met. Thirteen treatments, roughly a month at three a week, come to $3,662 approved and $732 for you, with no yearly cap. A Medigap Plan G brings that to $0. You keep paying the monthly Part B premium, and the Part D premium if you have a drug plan.

Who qualifies and which part pays

The dialysis benefit is for people with ESRD. Part B covers outpatient dialysis and doctors' services in a Medicare-certified facility or at home, including dialysis for acute kidney injury. Part A covers dialysis when you are admitted to a hospital: then the Part A deductible of $1,736 per benefit period applies, so a 4-day stay costs you $1,736, whatever the number of treatments during it. Your dialysis facility must provide the covered items and services itself or through an arrangement with another provider.

What 20% means over a year

Dialysis is one of the few Part B services you use several times a week for years, which makes the absence of a yearly cap on the 20% weigh heavily. The table counts treatments at the national base rate of $281.71; your facility's rate is adjusted by CMS for its area and for each patient, so your own approved amount can differ.

Drugs used for dialysis

Part B covers drugs for outpatient and home dialysis: heparin, the antidote for heparin when medically necessary, topical anesthetics and erythropoiesis-stimulating agents such as epoetin alfa or darbepoetin alfa to treat anemia related to ESRD. It also covers phosphate binders and other treatments that lower phosphorus in people with ESRD. Lab tests that are part of the dialysis services, heart monitoring and oxygen during treatment at a facility, and monitoring of your access site are covered too. Talk with your care team about any drug, including over-the-counter products.

Dental care before a transplant or during dialysis

Original Medicare does not cover most dental care, but dialysis for ESRD is one of the cases where it may pay for dental services closely related to the covered treatment. The dental page explains the exceptions.

Where the figures come from

The base rate is the 2026 figure of the CMS final rule for the ESRD prospective payment system. The coverage rules come from the medicare.gov dialysis services page. The 2027 base rate and the 2027 Part B deductible are not published yet.

Questions people on Medicare ask

Does Medicare pay for home dialysis equipment?

Yes. Part B covers the home dialysis machine, the water treatment system, a basic recliner and supplies such as alcohol, wipes, sterile drapes, rubber gloves and scissors, plus training for you and the person helping you, given by a Medicare-certified home dialysis training facility. Home visits by trained facility workers to check your equipment and water supply are also covered.

Does Medicare cover an ambulance to dialysis?

Only when traveling in any other vehicle could endanger your health, and only from home to the nearest dialysis facility and back. A scheduled non-emergency basic ambulance ride of 10 miles in the rest of Florida is approved at $375 and leaves you $75 with the deductible met. Rides for convenience are not covered.

What does Medicare not cover for dialysis?

Paid dialysis aides to help you at home, pay you or a helper lose during home dialysis training, a place to stay during your treatment, and blood or packed red blood cells for home dialysis unless they are part of a doctor's service. Other insurance you have may pay some of those costs.

Is the kidney doctor billed separately?

Yes. Outpatient doctors' services for ESRD are billed under Part B: after the $283 deductible you pay 20% of the approved amount. Medicare covers a face-to-face visit with your doctor or certain non-doctor providers once a month for home dialysis. During a hospital stay, the kidney doctor's monthly payment is based on the number of days you are in the hospital.
