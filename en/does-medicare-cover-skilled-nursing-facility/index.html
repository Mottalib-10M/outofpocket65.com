Does Medicare cover a skilled nursing facility?

Yes, for a limited time after a qualifying hospital stay, under Part A. Enter the number of days: the estimator applies the 2026 daily amounts and, if you choose one, your Medigap plan.

How this is calculated

Medicare Part A covers care in a skilled nursing facility (SNF) on a short-term basis, after a medically necessary inpatient hospital stay of at least 3 days in a row. The days are counted from the day you were admitted as an inpatient, not including the day you leave; time under observation or in the emergency room before admission does not count, even overnight. In each benefit period of 2026, days 1 to 20 cost you $0 once the Part A deductible is paid, and you do not pay that deductible again if you already paid it for the hospital stay in the same benefit period. Days 21 to 100 cost $217 each, and from day 101 you pay all costs, because Part A limits SNF coverage to 100 days per benefit period. A 30-day stay leaves you $2,170; the full 100 days, $17,360. Medigap Plans C, D, F, G, M and N pay that daily coinsurance in full, Plan K half and Plan L three quarters. Medicare Advantage plans may charge copays during the first 20 days.

The 3-day qualifying stay

Medicare covers SNF care only after a qualifying inpatient hospital stay: a medically necessary stay of at least 3 days in a row as an admitted inpatient. The hospital part is paid under Part A with its own deductible, $1,736 per benefit period in 2026, so a 4-day stay before the transfer costs you $1,736. The nursing facility days then follow in the same benefit period, and the deductible already paid is not charged a second time. Your doctor may not need a 3-day minimum stay in some situations described on the medicare.gov page.

Skilled care, not custodial care

Skilled care is nursing and therapy that can only be safely and effectively performed by, or under the supervision of, professionals or technical staff: managing and observing your condition, evaluating your care, therapy toward a health goal. A stay that is only help with daily living, without a need for skilled nursing or skilled therapy, is not what this benefit pays for, and non-medical long-term care is on the list of services Original Medicare does not cover.

Medigap and Medicare Advantage

The SNF coinsurance is one of the columns of the standardized Medigap chart: Plans C, D, F, G, M and N pay 100% of it, Plan K 50% and Plan L 75%, while Plans A and B do not pay it. A Medicare Advantage plan may charge copayments from the first day, including during the first 20 days; its Summary of Benefits gives the amounts and its yearly limit.

After the facility

When you go home, care can continue under the home health benefit if you qualify, with covered visits at $0, or as outpatient physical therapy under Part B at 20%. The 2027 daily coinsurance is not published yet; CMS announces it with the other Part A amounts in mid-November 2026.

Questions people on Medicare ask

Does time under observation count toward the 3-day stay?

No. Only days as a formally admitted inpatient count, starting with the day of admission and not counting the day of discharge. Hours or nights in the emergency room or under observation before admission are outpatient care and do not count, even if you stayed overnight. Ask the hospital whether you are an inpatient or an outpatient under observation.

What does a skilled nursing facility stay include?

A semi-private room, meals, skilled nursing care, physical and occupational therapy and speech-language pathology when needed for your health goal, medical social services, medications, medical supplies and equipment used in the facility, dietary counseling, and ambulance transportation to the nearest supplier of needed services that are not available at the facility when other transport would endanger your health.

How much do 45 days in a skilled nursing facility cost?

With Original Medicare alone, days 1 to 20 cost nothing and days 21 to 45 cost $217 each in 2026, so $5,425 in all. Medigap Plan G pays that coinsurance and leaves you $0; Plan K pays half and leaves $2,713.
