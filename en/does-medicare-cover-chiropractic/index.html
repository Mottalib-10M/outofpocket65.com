Does Medicare cover it?

Does Medicare cover chiropractic care?

Only in part. Part B pays for one thing a chiropractor does, the adjustment of the spine to correct a subluxation, and nothing else the chiropractor orders. The estimator prices a series of adjustments in your area with the 2026 fee schedule.

How this is calculated

Medicare Part B covers a single chiropractic service: manual manipulation of the spine to correct a subluxation, which Medicare describes as spinal joints that fail to move properly while the contact between them remains intact, done with the chiropractor's hands or with a small hand-held device called an activator. Everything else a chiropractor provides or orders is outside coverage and billed to you in full, including X-rays, massage therapy and acupuncture. For the covered adjustment you pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount after the $283 Part B deductible. At national 2026 rates, an adjustment of three to four spinal regions is approved $38, so each visit costs you $8 once the deductible is met, and a course of eight adjustments about $61. If the adjustment is your first Part B bill of the year, it falls entirely within the deductible and you pay the whole $38. Medigap pays the 20% on the adjustment under most letters but cannot pay for the excluded services, since they have no approved amount. The mini tool adds the excluded items you were billed to your share.

A narrow benefit with sharp edges

Chiropractic care, as Medicare describes it, covers techniques such as adjustments of the spine or other parts of the body to relieve pain. Medicare pays for a much smaller slice of that field. Part B covers adjustments of the spine by a chiropractor to correct a subluxation, done by hand or with an activator, and stops there. Adjusting a shoulder, a hip or a knee falls outside it, and so does every other service or test the chiropractor orders, massage and X-rays included: the clinic sets those prices.

In practice, a visit to a chiropractor can produce two kinds of lines on the same day: the covered spinal adjustment, which follows the Part B rule, and services Medicare does not pay for, which you settle in full. Asking the clinic which line each service belongs to before treatment starts avoids most surprises.

Price of the covered adjustment

The adjustment is paid under the physician fee schedule. The national 2026 amount for manipulation of three to four spinal regions is $38, and your 20% is $8 per visit once the deductible is met. Over a typical course, the amounts add up visit by visit:

Services that stay at full price

Medicare is explicit that it does not cover other services or tests a chiropractor orders, and it names X-rays, massage therapy and acupuncture. There is no approved amount for these items, so there is nothing for Medicare or a Medigap policy to share, and no cap on the price. Enter what you were billed for them in the mini tool to see the full cost of a course of care.

Acupuncture deserves a word, because Medicare does cover it in one situation, for chronic low back pain, under its own rules and providers. That coverage does not pass through a chiropractor's order; the acupuncture page explains it.

Supplemental coverage and the adjustment

Medigap pays after Medicare on the covered adjustment. With Plan G, twelve adjustments cost you $0 once the deductible is met; Plan L pays 75% of the coinsurance and leaves $23. A Medicare Advantage plan has to cover the spinal adjustment at least as Original Medicare does, and some plans add extra benefits; the plan's own documents give the copay.

Before a course of care begins

Ask the chiropractor whether they accept assignment, which keeps your share at 20% of the approved amount, and whether they participate in Medicare at all: a provider who has opted out is not paid by Medicare, even for the adjustment. Ask also which planned services are not covered and what they cost. Medicare's own advice is to ask why a service is recommended and what Medicare will pay for it, so that the bill matches what you expected.

Questions people on Medicare ask

Does Medicare pay for X-rays taken by a chiropractor?

No. Medicare states that it does not cover other services or tests a chiropractor orders, and it names X-rays first among them, along with massage therapy and acupuncture. Imaging ordered by your doctor or another provider to diagnose a medical problem is a different case: it falls under the Part B rules for diagnostic tests, at 20% after the deductible.

What is a subluxation in Medicare terms?

Medicare uses the word for spinal joints that fail to move properly while the contact between the joints stays intact. Correcting it by manual manipulation of the spine is the only chiropractic service Part B covers, whether the chiropractor uses the hands or an activator, a hand-held instrument. Adjustments of other parts of the body are not part of the covered service.

Can a chiropractor charge more than the Medicare-approved amount?

A chiropractor who accepts assignment takes the approved amount as full payment, so you owe only the deductible and 20%. One who does not accept assignment may charge up to 15% above the amount allowed for non-participating providers: at national rates your share of an adjustment rises from $8 to $13. Services Medicare does not cover have no limit at all.
